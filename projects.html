Systematic Underestimation in Defense Cost and Schedule Estimates

Independent Analysis

📄 READ REPORT 📊 VIEW SLIDES

Analyzed 31 major defense acquisition programs from GAO's 2024 and 2025 Weapon Systems Annual Assessments to test whether DoD's early cost and schedule estimates miss in a predictable direction, and by how much a decision-maker should adjust for it.

WHERE DOD SAYS COST GROWTH COMES FROM

Quantity
+$123.4B
Engineering
+$78.8B
Schedule
+$15.7B

The "Estimating" category, corrections to the estimate itself, was net negative (-$38.2B). Cost growth isn't simply "bad math" — but schedule, which is less distorted by quantity changes, still shows the clearest one-directional pattern.

  • Of 22 programs with schedule data, 20 slipped, 2 held steady, and none finished early — a one-directional pattern a sign test puts at about 1-in-a-million odds of being chance (p ≈ 0.000001)
  • Found schedule growth appears to precede cost growth: programs first estimated in 2016 or earlier show +51% schedule growth and +27% unit cost growth, while newer programs (2017+) have already slipped +29% on schedule despite unit costs still near flat (-1%)
  • Built an empirical reference class from these programs' actual outcomes: a new schedule estimate needs about 1.4x the original plan for 50% confidence of holding, and about 1.9x for 80% confidence, validated with a 20,000-iteration bootstrap
  • Case study: the Sentinel ICBM's first estimate was $94.1B; its own 2024 estimate rose to $129.0B (+37%) after a critical cost breach, while DoD's independent CAPE office priced the modified program at $170.6B (+81%) — a $41.6B gap between the inside and outside view
  • Cross-checked against DoD's own Selected Acquisition Report data to rule out "estimates are just bad at math": cost growth is attributed mostly to quantity and engineering changes, not estimating error
  • Recommended reporting schedule estimates as confidence ranges (50%/80%) rather than single dates, anchoring new estimates to reference-class history, and treating early schedule slips as a leading indicator for cost growth still to come

The Weeknd Mood Predictor — Predicting Spotify's Deprecated Audio Features from Raw Sound

Independent ML Project

🚀 LIVE APP 💻 VIEW CODE

Built a full ML pipeline to predict Spotify-style valence/energy scores directly from audio, after Spotify deprecated the API that used to provide these values (Nov 2024, no replacement).

HELD-OUT R² — ENERGY VS. VALENCE

Energy
0.203
Valence
0.009

Energy showed a real, validated signal. Valence performed no better than guessing the average — consistent with published MIR research showing arousal is far easier to predict from audio than emotional valence.

  • Engineered 22 audio features per track (tempo, spectral shape, harmonic/percussive decomposition, key correlation, 13 MFCCs) using librosa on 30-second preview clips, fully automated, no manual labeling
  • Trained on 99 tracks across The Weeknd's catalog (8 albums), evaluated on a 17-track fully held-out test set (After Hours deluxe) never seen during training or model selection
  • Compared 3 model families (Linear Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting) via 5-fold cross-validation, selected per-target on CV performance, with final scores reported only on the untouched held-out set, zero data leakage
  • Diagnosed why valence underperformed using SHAP analysis: clean, consistent signal for energy vs. noisy, inconsistent contributions for valence, confirming the audio signal itself was the bottleneck, not the modeling approach
  • Tested a fix: added lyric sentiment (VADER via Genius API, 116 tracks) as an extra valence feature. Diagnosed that VADER saturates near ±1.0 on ~90% of tracks, too coarse to help, and documented transformer-based sentiment as the evidence-backed next step
  • Shipped as a live, deployed Streamlit app with real-time iTunes lookup, on-the-fly feature extraction, model inference, and per-album color theming pulled from actual cover art
